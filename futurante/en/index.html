Futurante / definition

Futurante: actively making futures—changing how the present produces reality so that other realities can become possible.

It is an ethics of intention, imagination and action, not simply a way of predicting what comes next.

Developed through Maia Oazem's Futures Thinking research at PUC and the gira. Laborateliê, this work emerges from neomaqueerism: reactivating ancestral possibilities, living together through care and reciprocity, denormalizing the given, and cultivating these practices into culture.

Its horizon is Hope Culture — hope as a guide to action, not as passive waiting.

Core proposition

Another thing is necessary.

Cultural horizon

A culture that keeps the capacity to transform.

Three books,
one opening.

The project begins primarily in the dialogue between Le Guin, Maturana & Verden-Zöller, and Krenak.

  1. 01
    The Carrier Bag Theory of FictionUrsula K. Le Guin

    What if our stories centered carrying, gathering, sharing, and sustaining life rather than heroic conquest?

  2. 02
    Amar e Brincar · Love and PlayHumberto Maturana & Gerda Verden-Zöller

    What if cultural transformation meant changing the emotions, conversations, and relations we keep alive?

  3. 03
    Futuro Ancestral · Ancestral FutureAilton Krenak

    What if other futures also grow from knowledge and modes of living that dominant progress has marginalized?

The matristic is treated as an aspirational cultural horizon—not a claim of a scientifically proven universal past. Queer is used as a critical possibility for questioning which ways of living and futures the present recognizes as legitimate.

From ethics
to AI.

An alternative route from question to answer

AI can make a conventional future more efficient without asking which future it is helping to make.

Futurante Ethics changes the starting point: examine implicit futures, identify their strategies, imagine alternatives, understand the present, consider longer-term effects, and only then propose different actions.

This is an invitation to broaden choices, not to impose one ideal future. Evidence, inference and speculation must remain distinct; human judgment stays central.

A seven-stage decision pathwayClick each question to explore
01Implicit futures+

Which future is this request pursuing as if it were the only possible one?

Explore three hypotheses about the futures implicit in a request.

02Implicit strategies+

Which strategies seek conquest or control instead of cultivating life together?

Identify two or three strategies for each implicit future.

03Unimagined futures+

Which apparently impossible futures could challenge the norms behind this request?

Develop three counter-hypotheses and identify what makes them seem unthinkable.

04What else is necessary+

What emerging issues in the present require a different direction?

Connect present tensions to implicit strategies and alternatives.

05The state of the present+

What are lives and ecosystems already telling us about the world we make?

Read signals and evidence, and mark gaps and uncertainty.

06Speculative future states+

What will the original creation keep creating if nothing changes?

Explore possible second- and third-order effects without presenting them as fact.

07Other things possible+

What different actions could make a seemingly utopian direction pursuable now?

Propose three to five strategies linked to the present, with concrete first steps.

Principle I / VII

One Day, Tomorrow

Future-Making Agency

Core concept

The present is a continuous production of futures.

Meaning

Our actions and inactions participate in the making of tomorrow, even when we cannot fully control the outcome. The future is not something that simply happens to us; it is continuously made through the ways we act, relate, and choose.

Commitment

Take part intentionally in transformation without confusing agency with total control.

Central terms

Transformation · Intention · Responsibility

Futurante question

What future are we bringing into existence through what we do today?
Principle II / VII

The Mother Imagination World Order

Fertile Imagination

Core concept

Imagine futures through what we cultivate, not what we conquer.

Meaning

Move imagination away from possession, scarcity, and heroic conquest. Give narrative value to growing, tending, sharing, harvesting, and sustaining interconnected life. Abundance is not accumulation: it is life capable of regenerating when cared for.

Commitment

Treat care as a shared human capacity, not a gendered duty; cultivate life instead of replacing one heroic hierarchy with another.

Central terms

Cultivation · Care · Regenerative abundance

Futurante question

What does this story reveal about cultivation, care, and the interconnection of lives, rather than conquest and battle?
Principle III / VII

89 Seconds to Hope

Horizons of Hope

Core concept

Make desirable horizons visible to recognize and redirect change.

Meaning

Rather than a single countdown to a fatal midnight, imagine many walkable horizons of hope. Communities, territories, and living systems can identify different desirable possibilities and observe how present actions move toward or away from them.

Commitment

Make signals of proximity to desirable futures visible without reducing hope to a single metric or countdown.

Central terms

Plural futures · Orientation · Reflection

Futurante question

Which desirable futures are our actions bringing us closer to or further from—and how far have we walked?
Principle IV / VII

This Is Ourselves

Self–World

Core concept

We make the world, and the world remakes us with every action.

Meaning

We are not separate from the societies and ecosystems we participate in making. The effects of our choices move through communities, species, and generations. We are simultaneously the makers and the made of our realities.

Commitment

Recognize others as legitimate in coexistence while acknowledging uneven distributions of responsibility, power, and agency.

Central terms

Interdependence · Coexistence · Responsibility

Futurante question

Who are we becoming through the world we are making—and what kinds of people does that world make?
Principle V / VII

Empathy Is Also Biotechnology

Ecosystemic Sensitivity

Core concept

Perceiving the state of life is essential to transforming it with care.

Meaning

Attend to what humans, animals, plants, and environments reveal about their conditions of existence. Life communicates the state of the world through its changes. Bodily, social, and ecological sensitivities are meaningful ways of knowing, not inferior to digital technology.

Commitment

Listen without presuming; never turn empathy into extraction, manipulation, or projection. Create tools that strengthen our capacity to perceive and respond.

Central terms

Listening · Living signals · Situated knowledge

Futurante question

What is life around us communicating and foreshadowing about the world we are making?
Principle VI / VII

Healing Makers

Consequential Creator

Core concept

We are responsible for what our creations continue to create.

Meaning

Making never ends at delivery. Each artifact can reinforce or prevent other possibilities, producing reverberations across social, ecological, cultural, and temporal systems. We must ask what our creations will keep making possible—and what they will make more difficult.

Commitment

Consider direct and indirect consequences, repair harms, and transform the processes through which we create.

Central terms

Repair · Long-term effects · Accountability

Futurante question

What will our creations keep creating—and what are we choosing not to create?
Principle VII / VII

Reclaiming Technology

Common Technology

Core concept

Technology should expand possibilities for human and surrounding life.

Meaning

Technology is assembled from knowledge accumulated and transformed across generations. It is not an autonomous destiny, nor simply the property of those who currently control it. We can question, govern, change, share in its benefits, and refuse systems that determine our lives.

Commitment

Share technological benefits while protecting autonomy, authorship, cultural diversity, and consent; do not use the commons as an excuse for extraction.

Central terms

Collective agency · Shared benefit · Consent

Futurante question

How can we reclaim our ability to decide what our technologies do, whom they serve, and which worlds they help make?
